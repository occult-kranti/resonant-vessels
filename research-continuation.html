Round 1 · production + independent review

A simultaneous reference can identify common gain drift

Given known stable reference, separated channels, physical-time premise and common scalar gain, slope difference identifies target amplitude decay in the finite synthetic model.

Assumptions and limits

Known stable reference, ideal separated channels, shared scalar gain, physical-time premise. Generated PCM16, not physical recording.

  • Reference stability and shared gain are premises, not identified from corrected residuals.
  • Differential gain or unstable reference restores a nontrivial kernel; exact same-PCM examples with alpha 4 and 5 per second were executed.
  • The two channels already separate the signals; a one-microphone decomposition is not demonstrated.
  • Generated stereo PCM and digital full scale are not measured pressure, physical damping or energy.
  • No noise coverage or empirical calibration threshold was established.

Round 2 · production + independent review

A correction needs a full uncertainty budget

For fixed times and strictly positive supplied amplitude-error boxes, monotonic logs and linear OLS weights yield exact box endpoints. Explicit absolute nuisance bounds widen the contrast; absent bounds withhold a physical-alpha interval.

Assumptions and limits

Supplied positive envelope error boxes with declared reference/drift bounds. Not a statistical confidence interval.

  • This is deterministic interval arithmetic, not a confidence interval or an empirical coverage claim.
  • The supplied envelope-error guarantee does not follow from a recorded waveform, an AGC setting or a low residual.
  • A physical-alpha interpretation also assumes a single exponential, shared physical time and stated valid nuisance budgets.
  • The retained out-of-budget counterexample excludes true alpha; nominal width cannot compensate for a false bound.
  • The finite engineering ranges in these fixtures do not verify overflow behavior for arbitrary floating-point magnitudes.

Round 3 · production + independent review

Electrical voltage gain is not net energy gain

The specified passive lumped RLC circuit has capacitor voltage gain2 at resonance, positive source-off load work from initial storage, and a closed signed energy budget. Reactive apparent power is not mean real power.

Assumptions and limits

Ideal lumped RLC with series loop closed when source voltage is set to zero; signed electrical work and separately integrated losses.

  • A finite lumped linear simulation is not hardware calibration, empirical overunity testing or a proof about every electrical device.
  • Source off means ideal source voltage set to zero while the series loop remains closed; opening a physical switch would change this model.
  • Source waveform is sine starting at zero, stated explicitly in producer report.
  • Inductance/capacitance are constant here; moving geometry would add mechanical work and require a different boundary.
  • Numerical closure tolerance is not measurement uncertainty.

Round 4 · production + independent review

Magnetic support force needs source and mechanical work

In the declared linear-inductance prescribed-path model, maintained current supplies both field-energy increase and mechanical work. The 2mN ordinary upward magnetic force reduces a separately supported 1g test-pan indication with g unchanged and survives current reversal.

Assumptions and limits

Positive L(z), prescribed motion, maintained current and source-inclusive field/mechanical energy; static contact forces use unchanged g.

  • L(z) is a supplied finite-domain model, not a measured coil geometry.
  • The motion is prescribed; free dynamics, mechanical stage work and a moved load gravitational potential are not solved.
  • Static one-pan reduction assumes reaction support outside that pan. The independently derived complete static support sum cancels internal force only within the relative-coordinate model.
  • External fields, leads, heating, buoyancy and dynamic/field momentum remain possible measurement channels outside this model.
  • Current-even force is not a gravity identifier. No hardware, levitation or gravity change was measured.

Round 5 · production + independent review

Which observations could identify a gravity-like change?

Under the specified static two-parameter model, paired support/polarity observations across known masses identify a deliberately injected acceleration-like slope and constant bias. A mass-proportional ordinary bias exactly reproduces all raw observations, preventing empirical gravity identification without an independent bound.

Assumptions and limits

Synthetic contemporaneous static support contrasts, paired polarity and known masses; injected common acceleration identified only under constant even-bias model.

  • Injected delta_g is a hypothetical shared model deformation, not an observed anomaly or proposed new force law.
  • Known calibration, simultaneous supports, common static g and mass/polarity-independent even bias are premises.
  • The expanded inverse has domain3, rank2, nullity1, kernel(1,0,-1); repeating the same readout cannot resolve it.
  • The2microN support bound is supplied, not measured detector sensitivity.
  • The1e-12 numerical guard is tested on these finite fixtures and is not a universal floating-point enclosure certificate.
  • External fields, thermal/mass-correlated drift, electrostatic forces, air motion, leads and real apparatus calibration remain unresolved empirical channels.
