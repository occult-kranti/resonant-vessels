Part I

The Practitioners

Nikola Tesla Established record

1856–1943 · Electrical engineer · The archive’s north and south pole

Tesla is the figure this archive most has to rescue — from his admirers. The documented record is extraordinary without embellishment: the resonant transformer patents of 1896–1898, the Colorado Springs laboratory of 1899–1900 with its 142-foot mast and 3.5–4 megavolt discharges, and the diary entry of July 3, 1899, in which a ground-connected receiver waxed and waned as a storm receded and Tesla wrote, of stationary waves, “[This is of immense importance.]”1 His Earth-resonance scheme (US 787,412) was wrong as engineering — the planetary cavity’s Q of 4–6 cannot carry broadcast power — yet its qualitative intuition, that the Earth possesses electromagnetic eigenmodes in the single-digit hertz range, was vindicated fifty years later in the ionosphere-cavity sense by the Schumann resonances.2

The folklore is a separate archive in itself. The “FBI said Tesla was from Venus” story traces to the Bureau filing a contactee-cult newsletter — Margaret Storm’s Interplanetary Sessions Newsletter, received July 1957 — not endorsing it.3 The “missing death ray” dissolves on contact with John G. Trump’s 1943 examination: Tesla’s last fifteen years were “primarily of a speculative, philosophical, and somewhat promotional character,” and the hotel-safe mystery device resolved to an antique Wheatstone bridge.4

The quote audit

  • Misattributed “If you want to find the secrets of the universe, think in terms of energy, frequency and vibration” — traced to Ralph Bergstresser, a purple-plate vendor claiming a 1942 conversation.
  • Misattributed “3, 6, 9” vortex numerology — internet-era only; the Nikola Tesla Museum holds no record.
  • Fabricated “Earth is a realm” — a flat-earth-era fabrication.
  • Unverified “The day science begins to study non-physical phenomena…” — no source.
  • Documented “The Earth is responsive to electrical vibrations of definite pitch just as a tuning fork to certain waves of sound” — My Inventions, 1919.

Isaac Newton Established record

1642–1727 · Lucasian Professor · Chymist · The archive’s deepest file

Newton wrote roughly a million words of alchemy, in dated laboratory notebooks running from 1678 to 1696, now transcribed by the Chymistry of Isaac Newton project at Indiana University.5 His assistant Humphrey Newton recorded the working rhythm: “at spring & fall of ye Leaf… 6 weeks in his Elaboratory, the fire scarcely going out either Night or Day… The transmuting of Metals, being his Chief Design.” The papers descended through the Portsmouth family, were declined by Cambridge in 1888, scattered at Sotheby’s in 1936 — Keynes buying the alchemy, Yahuda the theology — and reassembled digitally two centuries later.6

The scholarly arc matters as much as the corpus. Betty Jo Dobbs read the alchemy as theology of nature; Richard Westfall proposed that a “profound conversion in 1679–80 under the combined influence of alchemy and orbital mechanics” produced action at a distance; William Newman’s decipherments — showing the cover-names resolving into reproducible bench operations — complicated both, noting that the 452 pages of lab notebooks mention God once, in material copied from George Starkey.7 Lawrence Principe’s formulation is this archive’s method: read the texts through laboratory replication, because “chymistry” was one discipline.

  • Documented Star regulus of antimony and the Net of Vulcan — made by Newton, replicated by Newman and Principe (filmed for BBC/NOVA).
  • Hypothesis Mercury poisoning behind the 1693 “distemper” — his hair carried elevated mercury; remains a hypothesis only.
  • Contested The alchemy-caused-gravity thesis (Westfall/Dobbs) — Newton’s alchemical gravity texts are mechanical, not occult-attractive.
  • Documented null No evidence Newton ever achieved the sophic mercury or the Stone — a falsified hypothesis preserved in a notebook, scientific content of the first rank.
Part II

The Bridge — Mind Meets Physics

Carl Gustav Jung Documented positions

1875–1961 · Psychiatrist · Alchemy’s psychological reader

Jung read alchemy as “projection of the unconscious individuation process onto matter” (Psychology and Alchemy, CW 12, 1944 — its dream series, remarkably, Wolfgang Pauli’s, anonymized). The four stages map to the work of the self: nigredo, “felt as melancholia… the encounter with the shadow”; albedo, the withdrawal of projections; rubedo, the coniunctio in which “the red and the white are King and Queen, who may also celebrate their chymical nuptials.” The lapis philosophorum is the Self: “base, cheap, immature, volatile… precious, perfect, solid.”8

With Pauli he produced the most intellectually serious occult–physics exchange on record: the 1952 Naturerklärung und Psyche, Jung’s “Synchronicity: An Acausal Connecting Principle” paired with Pauli’s essay on archetypal ideas in Kepler. Synchronicity — “a falling together in time… a hypothetical factor equal in rank to causality” — has never had widespread scientific approval; it is classified as pseudoscience, and reported cases are accounted for by probability and apophenia.9 This archive grades the empirical claim Untestable — no distinguishing prediction — while recording that its clinical phenomenology is real: 44% of therapists report synchronicity experiences (Roxburgh 2016).

The honest boundary was drawn by the founders themselves. Pauli insisted that “subjective properties of the observer or his psychological state are as irrelevant in the quantum mechanical laws of nature as in classical physics,” objected to Jung’s astrology experiment, and warned the psychoid archetype “cannot be used so lightly in the acausalities of microphysics.”

Roger Penrose Frontier program

b. 1931 · Mathematical physicist · Nobel laureate 2020

Penrose supplies this archive’s one serious, falsifiable quantum–consciousness program. The argument runs: Gödel-style, mathematical understanding is non-computable (The Emperor’s New Mind, 1989), so consciousness needs new physics; the candidate physics is objective reduction — superpositions collapse when the gravitational self-energy of the spacetime separation reaches τ ≈ ℏ/EG — and Hameroff’s candidate substrate is microtubule tubulin, orchestrated, collapsing in ~25 ms steps matched to 40 Hz gamma.10

The constraints are severe and are displayed, not hidden: Tegmark computed decoherence times of 10−13–10−20 seconds in the warm, wet brain against neural timescales of 10−3–10−1; Reimers found the scheme not biologically feasible; and at Gran Sasso, Donadi et al. (2021) ruled out the simplest parameter-free Diósi–Penrose model.11 Recent microtubule-optics results — exciton diffusion damped by anesthetics (Kalra 2023), room-temperature UV superradiance in tryptophan networks (Babcock 2024), epothilone B delaying anesthetic unconsciousness in rats (Khan 2024, single lab) — weaken blanket dismissal without demonstrating neural-timescale coherence or a causal role. Grade: Frontier.

Two cautionary appendices. The three-worlds ontology (physical, mental, Platonic mathematical) is Platonist metaphysics, labeled as such. And the observational claims for Conformal Cyclic Cosmology — concentric circles, Hawking points — were rebutted: the same anomalies appear in random skies. Evidence refuted; the cosmology remains elegant speculation.

Richard P. Feynman Established record

1918–1988 · Physicist · The archive’s epistemic standard

Feynman is here as the discipline itself. His resonance chapter (Feynman Lectures, Vol. I, Ch. 23) solves the driven damped oscillator once and deploys it across LCR circuits, the 10½-hour atmospheric tide after Krakatoa, infrared absorption in NaCl, paramagnetic resonance, the Mössbauer effect at Q ≈ 1010, and particle resonances — the same equation, a different carrier.12 Resonance, so stated, is exact: a natural frequency, a driving force, a damping, a quality factor. That precision is what makes it this archive’s correspondence criterion — and what convicts the phrase “raising your frequency,” which specifies no oscillator, no drive, no damping.

His stance on the paranormal was not ignorance but investigation: he personally examined ESP, isolation tanks, and Uri Geller, and found them unsubstantiated. The 1974 address Cargo Cult Science supplies the archive’s standing epigraph; the Challenger Appendix F supplies its enforcement clause: “reality must take precedence over public relations, for Nature cannot be fooled.”13

  • Misuse refuted “Nobody understands quantum mechanics” is a remark about counterintuitive formalism — not a license for mysticism. Feynman never connected quantum mechanics to consciousness.
  • Misuse refuted Sum over histories ≠ “all realities exist.” Decoherence is physical interaction, not consciousness-dependent.
Part III

The Margin

Stephen Mace Psychological register

b. 20th c. · New England sorcerer · Chaos-magick author

Mace, active since the 1980s and associated with the Illuminates of Thanateros, is the archive’s control case: a practicing occult author who makes no physical-frequency claims at all. Expositor of Austin Osman Spare, his works — Stealing the Fire from Heaven, Sorcery as Virtual Mechanics, Shaping Formless Fire, Taking Power, Seizing Power, Squeezing Being — define sorcery as “the art of capturing spirits and training them to work in harness,” then immediately translate: demons are “reflexes that generate uncontrollable moods, fantasies, and even actions”; sigils are intent compressed into glyph and repressed into the subconscious; Free Belief counterpoises conviction with its opposite to reach vacuity.14

The register is explicitly psychological and symbolic. Where the marketplace borrows physics vocabulary without instruments, Mace borrows nothing — and is therefore the rare occult writer this archive can quote without a warning label. His tradition’s testable cousin, goal priming and implementation intentions, is real psychology meta-analyzed at d ≈ 0.65 (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006); whether sigil practice itself works is Frontier — no RCT exists.
